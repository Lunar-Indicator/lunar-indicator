      How it helps

      ⚠️ Landing hazards

      Touchdown risks on steep crater slopes hidden by deep shadows are mitigated because the tool maps local terrain shadows to reveal clear, well-lit landing zones.

      🛡️ Crew survival

      Environmental control systems freezing from sudden power loss are avoided because the tool pinpoints guaranteed solar illumination windows to keep heaters and life support online.

      💰 Financial risk

      Multi-billion dollar hardware losses or premature mission aborts are prevented because the tool maximizes operational window overlaps between power and communications prior to launch commitment.

      Unlocking the Next Frontier for CLPS Missions

      How the Commercial Lunar Payload Services initiative is opening the south pole to science, industry, and eventually astronauts.

      🛰️ What is CLPS

      Commercial Lunar Payload Services is a NASA initiative that contracts private American companies to build robotic landers and deliver science instruments and technology payloads to the Moon's surface, rather than NASA building and flying its own landers. It is a key part of the foundation for Artemis, since many CLPS flights are scouting the same south-polar terrain that will later host crewed landings.

      Each task order covers the full trip end to end — payload integration, launch from Earth, and landing on the Moon — and the companies are selected competitively for individual missions rather than a single contractor flying every flight.

      🏢 Who is flying

      NASA opened CLPS to nine American companies in 2018 and added five more the following year, bringing the pool to 14 eligible vendors that can bid on delivery task orders as new payloads are ready to fly.

      🎯 Why the south pole

      CLPS deliveries are concentrated near the lunar south pole because that is where Artemis intends to land astronauts, and because the region's permanently shadowed craters are the best candidates anywhere on the Moon for water ice. Every lander that survives a south-polar touchdown and returns illumination or terrain data makes the next landing, crewed or robotic, a little less uncertain — which is exactly the kind of comparison this site is built to visualise.
